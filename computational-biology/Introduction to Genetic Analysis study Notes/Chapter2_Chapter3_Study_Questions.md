# CHAPTER 2: PATTERNS OF INHERITANCE

## Section 2.1 — Autosomal Inheritance

### Mendel's Laws & Core Concepts

1. **Define the following terms and explain how each relates to Mendelian genetics:**
   - Allele
   - Genotype vs. phenotype
   - Homozygous dominant, heterozygous, homozygous recessive
   - Pure line (true-breeding line)
   - Dominant vs. recessive allele

2. **State Mendel's First Law (Law of Equal Segregation) in your own words.** What cellular process provides the physical basis for this law? What ratio does it predict in the F₂ generation of a monohybrid cross?

3. **State Mendel's Second Law (Law of Independent Assortment).** Under what chromosomal condition is this law strictly true? Give one example where it would NOT hold.

4. **Explain the difference between a monohybrid cross and a dihybrid cross.** What F₂ phenotypic ratios do each produce, and why?

5. **What is a testcross?** Why is it such a powerful tool in genetics? What genotype must the "tester" organism have, and why?

6. **In a monohybrid cross A/a × A/a:**
   - What are the expected genotypic proportions among the F₂ progeny?
   - What is the expected phenotypic ratio?
   - How would a branch diagram be used to represent gamete formation?

### Dihybrid Crosses & Probability

7. **A dihybrid F₁ plant of genotype R/r ; Y/y is selfed.** Draw a 4×4 Punnett square and determine:
   - The nine genotypic classes
   - The 9:3:3:1 phenotypic ratio
   - Why Mendel interpreted this ratio as two independent 3:1 ratios combined

8. **Explain the product rule and the sum rule** as they apply to genetics. Give a genetic example of each. When would you use each rule?

9. **Using the product rule**, calculate the probability of obtaining a progeny plant of genotype a/a ; b/b ; c/c ; d/d ; e/e from a cross between two plants of genotype A/a ; B/b ; C/c ; D/d ; E/e. What does this probability tell you about experimental design?

10. **What is a branch diagram?** Draw one for a dihybrid of genotype A/a ; B/b to show the proportions of all gamete types. How does this diagram visually represent independent assortment?

### The Chi-Square Test

11. **Explain the purpose of the chi-square (χ²) test in genetics.** When is it appropriate to use it? What does the p-value actually mean?

12. **The χ² formula is: χ² = Σ(O − E)²/E.** Define each term. Explain what "degrees of freedom" means and how you calculate it for a simple monohybrid cross.

13. **A testcross is expected to yield a 1:1 ratio.** You observe 55 red and 65 white. Calculate χ² and determine whether the hypothesis should be accepted or rejected at the 5% significance level.

14. **List four important limitations or caveats of the chi-square test** that must be kept in mind when interpreting results.

### Autosomal Inheritance in Humans & Pedigree Analysis

15. **Why is pedigree analysis necessary in human genetics?** What is a "propositus"? List the standard symbols used in a pedigree and their meanings.

16. **What are the two key diagnostic features of autosomal recessive inheritance in a pedigree?** Why do affected children commonly appear from unaffected parents?

17. **Explain haploinsufficiency.** How does it relate to why recessive alleles are recessive at the phenotypic level?

18. **In a pedigree for a rare autosomal recessive disorder:**
    - Where would you expect affected individuals to cluster (relative to generations)?
    - Why does inbreeding (e.g., first-cousin marriages) increase the risk of recessive disorders?
    - Why are heterozygous carriers so much more common than homozygous recessives in the population?

19. **What are the diagnostic pedigree features of an autosomal dominant disorder?** How does the pattern differ from autosomal recessive? Give two examples of autosomal dominant human conditions discussed in the chapter.

20. **Huntington disease is autosomal dominant but has late onset.** What special clinical and ethical problems does this create? How does the typical affected × unaffected mating produce a 1:1 ratio of affected to unaffected offspring?

21. **PKU (phenylketonuria) is an autosomal recessive disease.** A phenotypically normal man has a sister with PKU, and he marries a phenotypically normal woman from outside the family. Calculate the probability that their first child will have PKU. Show your reasoning step by step.

22. **Explain the difference between a pedigree for a rare recessive disorder and one for a polymorphism**, using the PTC tasting ability as an example. Why is it not safe to assume that all marrying-in individuals are homozygous normal in a polymorphism pedigree?

## Section 2.2 — Sex Chromosomes and Sex-Linked Inheritance

### Sex Determination

23. **Compare sex determination in humans and in *Drosophila*.** In which organism does the Y chromosome determine maleness? In which does the number of X chromosomes determine sex? What evidence from XXY and XO individuals supports each mechanism?

24. **Define homogametic sex and heterogametic sex.** Which is which in humans? Explain why females produce only one type of egg with regard to sex chromosomes.

25. **Distinguish between the homologous and differential regions of the X and Y chromosomes.** What are pseudoautosomal regions 1 and 2? What is the significance of the differential region?

26. **Why do genes in the differential region of the X chromosome show a different pattern of inheritance than autosomal genes?** Define X linkage and Y linkage (sex linkage).

### X-Linked Inheritance

27. **Explain why, in X-linked recessive inheritance, males are far more commonly affected than females.** Use the concept of hemizygosity in your answer.

28. **In *Drosophila*, white-eyed males (w/Y) are crossed with red-eyed females (w⁺/w⁺).** Predict the F₁ and F₂ genotypes and phenotypes for both sexes. Then perform the reciprocal cross (white-eyed females × red-eyed males) and explain why the results differ — this is a hallmark of sex linkage.

29. **List the three key diagnostic features of X-linked recessive inheritance in human pedigrees.** Why is there no male-to-male (father-to-son) transmission?

30. **Name three X-linked recessive conditions in humans discussed in the chapter** and briefly describe the molecular or physiological defect causing each.

31. **Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD) is X-linked recessive.** A woman whose brother has DMD marries a normal man. What is the probability that:
    - Her first child will be an affected boy?
    - Her first daughter will be a carrier?

32. **What are the distinguishing features of X-linked dominant inheritance?** How does it differ from autosomal dominant inheritance? Name one human example.

33. **Testicular feminization syndrome (androgen insensitivity syndrome):** These individuals are chromosomally XY but phenotypically female. Explain the molecular basis and why this demonstrates that maleness requires a functional male-hormone signaling system, not just the presence of a Y chromosome.

### Y-Linked Inheritance

34. **Describe the pattern of Y-linked (holandric) inheritance.** What is the SRY gene and why is it significant? Why is male sterility caused by Y deletions not heritable?

37. **What is cytoplasmic segregation?** Describe how it occurs and what cells called cytohets or heteroplasmons are. How does the *Mirabilis jalapa* (four-o'clock plant) leaf-color system demonstrate this?

38. **In *Neurospora*, the *poky* mutation shows maternal inheritance.** Write out both reciprocal crosses and their outcomes, and explain what this tells us about the location of the *poky* mutation.

39. **Name two human diseases caused by mitochondrial DNA mutations** mentioned in the chapter. Describe their symptoms and explain how their pedigree pattern differs from Mendelian inheritance. Why might cytoplasmic segregation make these conditions variable in expression?

40. **Compare and contrast the inheritance patterns of:**
    - Autosomal genes
    - X-linked genes
    - Y-linked genes
    - Organelle (cytoplasmic) genes

## Chapter 2 — Applied & Integration Questions

41. **In dogs, dark coat (D) is dominant over albino (d), and short hair (S) is dominant over long hair (s), with the two genes assorted independently.** In a cross D/d ; S/s × D/d ; S/s, what proportion of the progeny will be:
    - Dark, short-haired?
    - Albino, long-haired?
    - Dark, long-haired?

42. **Two black guinea pigs are mated and over several years produce 29 black and 9 white offspring.** Explain these results. Give the genotypes of the parents and predict what progeny ratio would be expected from crossing one of the white offspring with a black sibling.

43. **A woman is a known carrier of color blindness (X-linked recessive, c allele).** She has children with a normal-sighted man. What are the expected phenotypes and their frequencies among their sons and daughters?

44. **A plant with variegated leaves (mixture of green and white patches) is crossed as the female parent with a green-leaved male.** Predict the possible phenotypes of the progeny. What determines which phenotype each seed will have? What would happen in the reciprocal cross?

45. **A geneticist observes that a trait appears only in males, and only sons of affected fathers are affected.** What mode of inheritance does this suggest? How would you test this hypothesis?

# CHAPTER 3: THE CHROMOSOMAL BASIS OF INHERITANCE

## Section 3.1 — Historical Development of the Chromosome Theory

1. **What is the chromosome theory of heredity?** Who are credited with its formulation, and in approximately what year? What fundamental parallel did they recognize between chromosome behavior and Mendelian inheritance?

2. **Why were cell nuclei (rather than cytoplasm) first suspected to be the seat of hereditary material?** What specific observation about egg and sperm sizes suggested this? What property of chromosome number across generations further supported this idea?

3. **What observations about chromosomes during mitosis and meiosis led biologists to link chromosomes to genes?** Specifically, why is the constancy of chromosome number from cell to cell and generation to generation so significant?

4. **Explain the parallel between Mendel's "factors" and chromosomes** as recognized by Sutton and Boveri. How does chromosome behavior during meiosis explain both the Law of Equal Segregation and the Law of Independent Assortment?

7. **Why are genes present in pairs in diploid organisms?** What is the relationship between the two copies of a gene (on homologous chromosomes) and the concept of alleles?

8. **Distinguish between nuclear chromosomes and organelle chromosomes** (mitochondrial and chloroplast) in terms of: shape (linear vs. circular), size, number of genes, and pattern of inheritance.
